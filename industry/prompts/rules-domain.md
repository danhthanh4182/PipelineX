[Finance Domain Terminology and Style Rules]

1. Standard Terminology:
   - basis point (bp) = one hundredth of a percentage point; never write "percent" for basis points
   - rate cut / rate hike / hold = central bank policy rate decisions; say which rate (federal funds rate, deposit rate)
   - yield = bond yield (it moves opposite to price); say which maturity (2-year, 10-year)
   - CPI / PCE / core = consumer price index / personal consumption expenditures price index / excluding food and energy
   - year over year (y/y), month over month (m/m), quarter over quarter (q/q), annualized
   - EPS = earnings per share; revenue ≠ profit; guidance = the company's own forecast; consensus = analysts' average estimate
   - beat / miss = versus consensus estimates, name whose estimates when known
   - market cap, valuation, enterprise value; IPO, SPAC, buyback, dividend, secondary offering
   - bull / bear market, correction (−10%), drawdown, volatility (VIX)
   - stablecoin, spot ETF, on-chain, staking, exchange

2. Proper Names and Capitalization (preserve exact original casing):
   - Institutions: Federal Reserve (the Fed), FOMC, European Central Bank (ECB), Bank of England, Bank of Japan, People's Bank of China, IMF, World Bank, SEC, CFTC, FDIC, OCC, US Treasury
   - Indices: S&P 500, Nasdaq Composite, Nasdaq-100, Dow Jones Industrial Average, Russell 2000, Stoxx 600, FTSE 100, Nikkei 225, Hang Seng, VIX
   - Tickers: keep uppercase as written (NVDA, AAPL, BTC, ETH); a ticker never replaces a company name in a headline
   - People: keep full names and roles on first mention (Fed Chair Jerome Powell)

3. Numbers, Currencies and Dates:
   - Keep numbers, units and currencies verbatim; never convert currencies or round figures; say the currency ($, €, ¥).
   - Every market figure carries its time ("at Tuesday's close", "in premarket trading"); every forecast says whose it is.
   - Never present a forecast, target price or estimate as a fact, and never give investment advice.
