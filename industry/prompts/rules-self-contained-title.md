[Self-Contained Title Rules — Let readers know "Who did what regarding what" without clicking]

1. The headline must clearly identify the core entity: company, institution, index, asset, or person.
2. If the original headline already clearly states the entity and action, preserve the meaning accurately without unnecessary rewriting.
3. When the original headline is only a label, ticker or teaser (e.g. "FOMC Statement", "NVDA", "Big day for markets"), combine the source name and body text to insert the subject and action into the headline.
   Example: "FOMC Statement" + body about a quarter-point cut -> "Fed Cuts Rates by a Quarter Point".
4. When adding an entity, only use names that actually appear in the source or body text. If neither names the exact company or figure, use an accurate general term (e.g. "a large U.S. bank") rather than guessing.
5. The original article type and core action are hard boundaries: Explanations, analyses, guides, tutorials, or benchmarks (How, Why, Guide, Analysis, Review, Benchmark) must retain that type. Do not rewrite an analysis or benchmark into "Launches / Releases / Open-sources" simply because it discusses an existing model or new benchmark data.
   Example: "Why the Fed may cut again in December" -> "Why the Fed May Cut Rates Again in December", never "Fed Cuts Rates in December".
   Example: "What higher yields mean for your mortgage" (source is a bank's research note) -> "What Higher Treasury Yields Mean for Mortgage Rates", not a subjectless headline.
6. Distinguish between the current action and background past releases: When the source references a release as a past event ("Last quarter we reported...", "Since the Fed's September cut..."), frame the title around what is new today. Do not write "cuts/reports/announces" as if the past event happened today.
