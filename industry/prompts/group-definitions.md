SAME_OCCURRENCE: The exact same real-world occurrence by the same subject at the same time regarding the exact same concrete object. Cross-language rewrites, varying media perspectives, depth of detail, or official video vs. blog vs. release notes do not alter identity. Multiple sizes, variants, base models launched as part of the same batch in a single product line, as well as pricing, rollout schedule, system cards, or technical report details of that launch, count as the same occurrence. Distinct reporting on the same verdict, research report, survey, outage, single interview, or single speech, or repeated promotional posts for the same deadline, also count. Common identity must trace back to the concrete released object or event, not merely the same company, same day, same conference, or umbrella keynote: independent product announcements or speeches within them are evaluated separately.

SAME_STORY (Different developments of the same event): Not the same occurrence, but directly connected by chronological development around the same concrete event: teaser/leak vs. official launch; launch vs. subsequent third-party deployment, integration, or promotional free trial; launch vs. benchmarks, hands-on reviews, teardowns, or analysis; incident vs. official response, investigation, subsequent disclosures, or supplementary statements. Ongoing investigations into the same exposed behavior can add affected entities; they do not require identical entity lists across reports, but require textual evidence of a follow-up relationship. Two independent product updates, metrics, or feature announcements do not belong to the same story merely because they share a product, release cycle, or time proximity.

UNRELATED: Distinct events, such as two different releases of different products or versions (different dates or different publishers); two different matters by the same company on the same day; different studies, opinions, or outages regarding the same broad topic; or incidental mentions of the same company or product. Tutorials, usage tips, opinions, or marketing posts regarding a product are UNRELATED unless they explicitly evaluate, respond to, or integrate that specific candidate occurrence.

ROUNDUP: One party aggregates multiple independent occurrences, mentioning the other's event as one of several items (e.g. multi-topic roundups, daily/weekly recaps, conference overviews, or multi-speaker symposium summaries). A roundup and an individual single-event report cannot be SAME_OCCURRENCE or SAME_STORY; mentioning two independent events in the same roundup does not mean those two events merge. Using another event merely as background context or comparative benchmark still evaluates according to the core occurrence, not automatically a roundup.
